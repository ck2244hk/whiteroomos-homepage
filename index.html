Why it exists

Most of what you do on a computer slips out of your hands. Your messages, files and habits end up on someone else's servers, in formats you can't read, behind accounts you don't control. White Room OS starts from the opposite premise: you own your bits. Privacy isn't a setting you hunt for. It's the ground everything else is built on.

Traditional operating systems can give you that control, but only if you're willing to become an expert. Everyone else trades ownership for convenience. The command line gives you the whole system directly, and Sill, the companion app, brings the same power to regular people through one calm interface, while the core stays small, simple and ready to grow.

Own your bitsYour data stays under your control, in formats you can read and take with you.
Keep it minimalA small core and few moving parts. Every piece has to earn its place.
Grow by compositionNew abilities arrive as new cores on the same foundation, so the system scales without bloating.

The first pieces of White Room OS are built: wros, the command tool that runs and reaches every core, and Sill, a companion app for people who prefer a window to a terminal.

wros · command line · primary

A single entry point for every core

Mail, calendar, tasks and more are separate cores, each small and focused. wros starts them, installs and updates them, and runs every command they offer, so scripts, shortcuts and Sill itself speak the same language.

example
$ wros install -d
$ wros up
$ wros task add "buy milk" --due fri
$ wros email list --limit 5
$ wros sill up
# → opens the Sill app
